nobody in my course requires me to know calculus at the level i study it. it won't show up in a CPTS exam. it probably won't directly help me root a machine.

i do it anyway.

## professor leonard

if you don't know paul leonard's calculus lectures on youtube — find them. they are the most patient, thorough mathematics teaching i've ever seen. the man takes nothing for granted. he explains *why* before he explains *how*.

that approach is rare. most instruction jumps straight to the method and trusts you to just absorb the intuition by osmosis. leonard doesn't.

i work through his lectures slowly. i pause. i work the problems myself before he does. i take notes in my diary, not just on screen.

## HC verma

*Concepts of Physics* is the book. if you grew up in india trying to understand physics properly, you know it.

what i love about verma: the problems are genuinely hard. not hard in a gotcha way — hard in a "you actually have to understand the physics" way. you can't formula-hunt your way through them.

## why i actually do this

honestly: it keeps a part of me alive that the security grind doesn't touch.

security work is methodical, adversarial, technical. i love it. but mathematics and physics are something else — they're about understanding structure at a fundamental level. about why things are the way they are.

there's a loose dream in me of understanding aerospace someday. orbital mechanics, propulsion, structures. i don't know if it'll ever become anything. but i'm not ready to let it go.

so i keep studying. professor leonard on tuesdays. verma problems when i'm stuck or need a different kind of thinking.

it's the most honest thing i do.
